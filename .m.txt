fix(guides): the office build contradicted its own advice

bureautique-etudes-90k listed a Ryzen-class i5-12400F at 33,900 DA next to an
RX 580 8GB at 32,900 DA. Both complaints about that build were correct, and
there was a third nobody had spotted yet.

What was wrong
- The build cost 143,200 DA while the guide was named 90k and its own text said
  the 90k budget "ne sort plus". The claim was false by 53,200 DA.
- The 12400F has the F suffix, so it has no integrated graphics and cannot output
  a signal without a card. The RX 580 was therefore not optional garnish, it was
  a 32,900 DA dependency - and on top of an already overpriced CPU.
- The guide's own prose says the graphics card "ne sert presque a rien" for
  office work and that the CPU is "large". The parts list then spent 66,800 DA
  of 143,200 - 47% of the machine - on exactly the two components the guide
  identifies as the least important. The writing was right and the build
  contradicted it.
- Together the CPU and the card cost more than the rest of the machine combined,
  for a use case that needs neither.

What it is now
  cpu-r3-3200g        13,000   Ryzen 3 3200G, integrated Vega GPU
  cooler-am1204        4,400
  mobo-a520m          13,700
  ram-vengeance-16-d4 23,900
  ssd-nvme-512gb      14,500
  case-nx400          10,200
  psu-450-b            5,200
                     -------
                      84,900 DA

An APU with integrated graphics, so no discrete card is needed at all. That is
not a compromise, it is the correct answer for office and study work, and it is
the same lesson the "F suffix" section was trying to teach - now applied instead
of contradicted. It also lands genuinely under the 90k the guide is named for,
so the title is finally honest.

Prose corrected too
Seven paragraphs and pitfalls still argued from the removed parts: the 550 W
supply recommendation (unnecessary with no GPU), the 400 W load calculation, the
used-RX-580-as-the-plan, and the case-length caveat. All rewritten to match the
new build. One mention of the i5-12400F is kept deliberately, as the
counter-example that shows the 66,800 DA mistake being avoided.

English synced, with a parity check
The English guide listed the old parts, so the two language versions of one
build showed different machines - the worst kind of bug here, because neither
version looks wrong on its own. Fixed, and scripts now assert identical parts
arrays across all 19 guides in both languages: PARITY OK.
